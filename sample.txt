Chapter 1: The Storm

On the western edge of a small coastal village stood an old lighthouse called Greywatch. It had guided ships safely through the rocky waters for more than eighty years.

The lighthouse keeper was a quiet man named Elias. Every evening, just before sunset, Elias climbed the 137 steps to the lantern room and switched on the great rotating lamp.

One autumn night, a powerful storm moved toward the coast. The villagers closed their windows and brought their boats onto the shore. Elias, however, remained inside Greywatch.

At 11:40 PM, the electricity failed.

Elias climbed the stairs with an oil lamp and discovered that the main light had stopped working. He remembered an old emergency generator stored in the basement.

Chapter 2: The Missing Key

Elias hurried downstairs, but the generator room was locked.

The key was normally kept in a wooden box near the entrance. When Elias opened the box, the key was missing.

He searched the keeper's office and found an old notebook belonging to the previous lighthouse keeper, Margaret Vale. On one page, Margaret had written:

"Spare generator key: behind the third stone beneath the eastern window."

Elias went outside into the storm and found the eastern window. Behind the third loose stone was a small brass key.

He unlocked the generator room and started the machine. After several attempts, the generator finally came to life.

Chapter 3: The Ship

At 12:15 AM, Greywatch's lamp began rotating again.

Far out at sea, a cargo ship named North Star was struggling against the storm. Its navigation equipment had been damaged, and the crew could barely see through the rain.

The captain saw the light from Greywatch.

Using the lighthouse as a reference point, the captain changed the ship's course by twelve degrees. The North Star avoided the rocks and continued safely toward the harbor.

Chapter 4: Morning

By sunrise, the storm had passed.

The villagers gathered near Greywatch and thanked Elias. The captain of the North Star later sent him a letter saying that the lighthouse had saved twenty-three lives.

Elias placed the letter inside Margaret Vale's old notebook.

He then added one final sentence beneath her instructions:

"Some lights are old, but they still know the way home."

Greywatch continued to shine every night for many years.
